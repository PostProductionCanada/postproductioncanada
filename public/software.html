💻 Editing Software

From film strips to timelines, and what each tool is doing now.

Editing started as a hands-on craft and became a computer craft in a few years. This page tells that story, then lists the main tools for picture, sound and visual effects, with what is new in each. Versions and news were read from the makers' own pages on November 19, 2026. Icons are simple emoji, not company logos.

💻 A short history: from film to digital

  1. Before 1971 🎞️

    Film editing was a mechanical craft: Editors cut and joined strips of celluloid by hand, on tools such as the Moviola and the flatbed.

    IATSE Local 695
  2. 1971 🖥️

    The CMX 600, a joint venture of CBS and Memorex, is the first truly non-linear Editor. It has two monitors, a light pen and disk packs the size of washing machines.

    Wikipedia
  3. 1984 💽

    Lucasfilm's EditDroid, built on a bank of LaserDiscs, shows at NAB. It lays the groundwork for bins and a timeline.

    Simon Says
  4. 1989 🧲

    The EMC2 and then Avid's Avid/1, the first Media Composer, arrive within weeks of each other. Lightworks is also created this year, in the UK, by Paul Bamborough, Nick Pollock and Neil Harris.

    Wikipedia
  5. 1991 🇨🇦

    Discreet Logic is founded in Montréal. Its Flame, Inferno and Smoke systems become a mainstay of visual effects and finishing. Lightworks appears in a London facility the same year.

    Wikipedia
  6. 1992 to 93 📀

    Storage is the barrier. Until 1992 a Mac could reach only about 50 GB at once. By February 1993 a Disney Channel team had Media Composer reaching over seven terabytes, and long-form feature editing became possible. Within a year thousands of systems had replaced 35mm editing equipment in studios and TV stations.

    Wikipedia
  7. 1999 🤝

    Autodesk agrees to buy Discreet Logic for about US$520 million. The unit stays in Montréal.

    Tech Monitor
  8. 2014 🇨🇦

    Blackmagic Design buys eyeon Software of Toronto, maker of the Fusion compositor. Fusion now lives inside DaVinci Resolve.

    fxguide
  9. 2026 🤖

    Agentic and AI-assisted tools arrive in every major Editor, and Premiere, Resolve, Media Composer and Final Cut all shipped major updates this year.

    See below
About Lightworks. It is often remembered as Canadian, but every source we found says it was created in the UK in 1989 and first appeared in a London facility in 1991. We could not find a source for a Canadian origin. If you have one, send it to hello@postproductioncanada.ca and we will add it.

🟩 VFX Editors and the VFX houses

A VFX Editor sits in editorial and is the link between the picture edit and the VFX houses. They track every effects shot, send the vendors what they need, and cut the returning versions into the timeline so the Editor and director can judge them. The role is described in detail in ScreenSkills' VFX Editor skills checklist and in Editor Evan Schiff's turnover guide.

  1. 1Cut with tempsThe Editor cuts with storyboards, previs and rough placeholders. A VFX Editor or Assistant builds quick temp composites, often called slap comps.
  2. 2TurnoverThe VFX Editor tracks every effects shot with an ID, then packages plates, frame handles, reference video, retime data, notes and count sheets for the vendor.
  3. 3Vendor worksA VFX house (Rodeo FX, Image Engine, Folks and others) builds the shot in tools such as Nuke, Houdini, Flame or Fusion.
  4. 4Versions come backThe VFX Editor cuts each version into the timeline, checks plate, length, colour and handles, and reviews it with the Editor and director.
  5. 5Notes and finalsNotes go back to the vendor. Final shots are checked and sent to the colour finish (the DI), with the right version of every shot.

Where the VFX houses are

📍 British Columbia Image Engine (founded in Vancouver in 1995, merged with Cinesite in 2015), The Embassy, Zoic Studios, plus Vancouver outposts of Wētā FX, ILM, Framestore, DNEG, Pixomondo, Sony Imageworks, Digital Domain and Scanline VFX (owned by Netflix).

📍 Québec Rodeo FX (Montréal, with a Québec City studio), plus Montréal branches of Framestore, DNEG, MPC, Pixomondo, Sony Imageworks, Digital Domain, Crafty Apes and Scanline VFX.

📍 Ontario Spin VFX, Rocket Science VFX, Soho VFX, Folks VFX and Guru Studio in Toronto, a Rodeo FX studio (opened in Toronto's Liberty Village in 2022), plus DNEG, Tippett Studio and Ghost VFX.

Studio lists are from VFX Voice's guide to VFX in Canada. Studios open, merge and close often, so check each one's own site.
