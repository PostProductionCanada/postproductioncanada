When Guillermo del Toro's *Frankenstein* needed 950 effects shots, much of that work went to Mr. X, a Toronto studio that has handled del Toro's effects since *The Shape of Water*, according to an Autodesk write-up. The ship scenes were filmed on a parking lot at Netflix's Toronto studio, as the film's Toronto-based production designer told the Motion Picture Association. A film shot in Toronto with effects made in Toronto is the simple case. The more interesting ones are where the shoot and the effects split apart.

## The credits that follow the work

Several Canadian credits are tied to where the labour happens, not where the camera was.

- **Federal:** the Production Services Tax Credit pays 16% of qualified Canadian labour, with no effects-specific bonus.
- **British Columbia:** the basic Production Services credit is 36% for productions that start principal photography after December 31, 2024, and the DAVE credit adds 16% of BC labour on digital animation, visual effects and post-production. Creative BC says productions with only VFX or post-production work done in BC can access the basic credit and DAVE, if eligible.
- **Ontario:** the OCASE credit is 18% of eligible Ontario labour on computer animation and special effects, claimed on top of Ontario's film or production services credit. The effects house must be under contract with the producer, and eligibility depends on several tests.
- **Québec:** the production services credit is 25% of qualified spend, with an extra 16% on qualified labour for computer-aided special effects and animation. In 2024 the base rate rose from 20% to 25%, but a 65% cap came with it, which the union IATSE called a sudden and significant cut and linked to layoffs.

## Why a film can shoot elsewhere and still do its effects here

Because BC lets effects-only work claim the credits, a production can film somewhere else and still book its visual effects in Vancouver. One Vancouver studio guide notes that ILM, DNEG, Digital Domain and Sony Pictures Imageworks all run studios there, with recent examples including *Tron: Ares*, *The Odyssey* and *The Fantastic Four: First Steps*. Treat that guide as a secondary source, since it is a directory rather than a credits database.

## Why a film can shoot here and take its effects elsewhere

Canada is not the only place chasing this work. Ireland has just raised its VFX credit to 40% for projects spending at least €1 million on qualifying effects, and the Irish government says the move is meant to compete with the UK, France, New Zealand and Canada. The UK has also made VFX a priority in its own credit. And a cap like Québec's changes the maths for studios in Montréal.

## What to keep in mind

Credits are labour-based and come with caps, tests and yearly budget changes. Studio ownership and long relationships with directors matter too: del Toro's repeat work with Mr. X is the clearest example here. If you are planning around any figure, confirm it with Creative BC, Ontario Creates, CAVCO or Revenu Québec first.
