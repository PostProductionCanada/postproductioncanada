🧭 Post Supervisors: the people who run the schedule

If the Editor owns the cut, the post supervisor owns everything around it. Reel Canada describes them as the main line of communication between the producers, director, Editors, sound Editors, outside facilities and the production accountant. It also calls the role not creative, yet integral to delivery.

What they manage
The post schedule and budget, vendor deals, and the deliverables list and specs for each platform, plus QC through to delivery, according to ScenePaper's job description. Frame.io's guide says they are ideally on board before the shoot, helping set up the cutting room workflow, hiring crew and choosing outside vendors.
Why they get special DGC attention
Under the 2023–25 DGC Ontario schedule (ON21), post supervisor and coordinator functions may only be done by a Guild member, a non-member with a bona fide producer credit, or a full-time employee of the producer's parent company. That keeps the job inside the guild unless the person has real producing duties.
Training pipeline
The same Ontario schedule has a letter agreeing that a Tier A production doing post in Ontario engages a trainee from one of three streams (Picture Editing, Sound Editing, or Post Production Supervision and Coordination) for six weeks or the length of post, whichever is less. A matching letter appears in the 2026–28 Maritimes agreement.
Who represents them
DGC Ontario names Post-Production supervisors among its categories. Reel Canada's page for the role lists the DGC councils in Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario, Newfoundland and Labrador and the Maritimes. We have not confirmed coverage in BC or Québec.

How they start

  • Reel Canada says most Post-Production supervisors have at least four years in editing, sound or a management role, and most come in as production assistants. Reel Canada
  • Backstage describes two routes: rise inside one post department such as picture or sound editorial, or go from post assistant to post coordinator to supervisor. Backstage (US)
  • A post supervisor interviewed on YouTube (Chloé Bellande) says the usual path is from post coordinator to supervisor within the same show, because the producer already trusts you. YouTube interview
  • Caroline Dobbe, a post supervisor on The Boys, told Careers in Film that she learned the technical side on the job, and that knowing editing software is not necessary for the role. Careers in Film
  • The College of the North Atlantic's one-year Post-Production certificate in Newfoundland and Labrador includes a Post-Production Supervision course. See the Education page.

What the agreement says vs. what the job often becomes

On paper
The DGC Core Agreement says the Post Production Supervisor is engaged by the producer to coordinate, facilitate and supervise all Post Production planning and execution, including logistics, workflow, budgeting and scheduling, in consultation with the producer and department heads (Core Agreement 11.37, 2023–25 text).
In practice, per working supervisors and guides
Frame.io calls the role a circus ringleader: when one phase of the schedule changes it ripples into the others, so supervisors reassign crew, update schedules and move money between budget lines, and ideally start before the shoot to set up the workflow and cutting room and help hire crew. The post supervisor Chloé Bellande says much of the job is tracking visual effects and quality-checking the delivery list, which can include material for the marketing department such as trailers and posters. A LinkedIn guide written around Netflix productions lists bidding vendors, setting up the camera data and dailies plan with the DIT, and preparing the wrap memo. Storiara notes that on bigger shows the coordinator handles daily logistics while the supervisor owns the plan, budget and vendor relationships.
The gap
The agreement defines the role in a sentence. The sources describe a job that reaches into scheduling, vendor deals, visual effects tracking and delivery. Where an individual supervisor's duties start and stop is set by the deal and the production. If you supervise post and have seen the difference between the contract and the work, tell us and we will check it before adding.

More sources: Backstage, Careers in Film, Storiara, LinkedIn guide, How to Become a Post Production Supervisor (YouTube). Backstage's article is US-focused and says supervisors there do not belong to a union, which is not the case in much of Canada.

Sources: Reel Canada, ScenePaper, Frame.io, DGC Ontario Schedule 5 (CMPA). If you supervise post and know where the job gets tricky under the agreement, tell me and we'll check it and add it.

How to read this

Coverage depends on the production. Many Editors work non-union, and some work under more than one agreement. "Not confirmed" means we couldn't verify it from a public source. Check with the local, guild or association before you rely on anything here. The Canadian Cinema Editors is a professional association, not a union, so it appears only under "post community".
