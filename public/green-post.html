🌍 Green Post-Production

What the DGC, the provinces and broadcasters are doing about the footprint of screen work, and what it means for post, from editing rooms to render farms.

What Canada is doing

🇨🇦 Union Editors tips

DGC Green

Directors Guild of Canada

The guild's climate hub, with Top Tips for each department including Editors, a case study from the art department of the thriller Bed Rest, a DGC Materials Carbon Calculator, and a directory of Canadian and international programs. The DGC Green Award went to Peak Everything at the 2025 DGC Awards.

🇨🇦 📍 BC Province

Reel Green

Creative BC

Founded in 2006 at the BC Film Commission. It offers tools, training, vendor lists and clean-energy and circular-economy toolkits. A strategic plan for 2026 to 2029 is published.

🇨🇦 📍 ON Province

Ontario Green Screen

Ontario Creates

Launched in 2020 with a framework for measuring and reporting environmental impact, best-practice guides and a map of grid tie-in points for clean power on location.

🇨🇦 📍 QC 📍 MB Province

Rolling Green / On Tourne Vert

Québec

Québec's initiative with a sourcebook of providers. DGC Green's resource list also links Manitoba Film & Music's Reel Green and the IATSE Green Committee.

🇨🇦 Broadcaster

Greening our Story

CBC/Radio-Canada

A five-year plan from 2021 committing in-house productions to the albert carbon calculator. In 2022 it expanded to require independent producers with budgets over $400,000 to use albert, with a sustainability plan expected on smaller projects, including Post-Production.

🇨🇦 📍 BC Animation Compute

Drawn to Change: animation toolkit

Producing for the Planet

Launched September 22, 2026 with support from Rocket Fund Canada and Reel Green. It includes a guide and a carbon calculator built for animation, where emissions come mostly from render farms and compute hours, studio energy and distributed teams.

🇨🇦 Data

The footprint numbers

Producing for the Planet

A 2021 Sustainable Production Alliance report that included Canadian productions estimated a drama hour at 35 to 81 tonnes of CO2e, with tentpole features above 1,000 tonnes. The page also links a Telefilm study of Canada's audiovisual sector footprint by Green Spark Group.

🇨🇦 📍 ON Event

Sustainable Production Forum

SPF25, Toronto

A ten-year-old industry forum. The 2025 Toronto edition launched the Green Frame Progress Report and the expanded DGC Materials Carbon Calculator, with partners including Ontario Creates, Creative BC and IATSE 891.

🇨🇦 Post International

VFX and the carbon question

VFX Voice

Notes that good figures on VFX's footprint barely exist. It cites a 2016 BAFTA finding that animation's emissions come mostly from production offices and Post-Production, and describes a studio that moved fully to cloud on a renewables-backed provider. A UK study adds that data movement and cloud rendering are poorly measured. This is not Canadian reporting.

🎞️ From film to digital: what changed

Post used to run on chemistry. Here is what the sources say about the footprint of film, and what happened when it went away. This covers film itself. Digital has its own footprint in storage, rendering and streaming, and that is the next chapter we plan to research.

Film versus digital, by the numbers

UCLA Institute of the Environment and Sustainability, 2021 (a student practicum report for HBO)

The study compared an hour of unedited footage. It found that 35mm film needs about 180 times more energy and 224 times more water than digital capture on SD cards, and produces 3.4 times more CO2 equivalent and 1.5 times more hazardous waste: 561 kg of CO2e for film against 165 kg for digital. It is a student report, and we have not checked how it counts the energy of storing and rendering digital files, so read it as one estimate.

What came out of the lab

Ohio State University Environmental Health and Safety

Film processing waste can contain silver, which the US Environmental Protection Agency treats as a toxic heavy metal. The university's fact sheet gives silver concentrations from 5 to 12,000 mg/L depending on the stage, says spent solutions and rinse waters can count as hazardous waste, and says films and negatives themselves may need to be managed as hazardous waste. Silver recovery units are one way to treat it.

Less film, less silver in the sewers

PetaPixel, 2023 (about still photography, not cinema)

A photography article reports that in 2006 Swedish authorities found silver pollution in Stockholm's sewers had halved over five years, which they attributed to the decline of film photography and the rise of digital. It is a still-photography example, but it shows the direction of travel.

Kodak film sales fall 96 percent

NPR, August 8, 2014

NPR reported that Kodak's motion-picture film sales had dropped 96 percent over the previous decade, at the moment Hollywood studios agreed to keep buying a set amount of film so Kodak would keep making it. Most productions had already moved to digital.

Canada's film labs close

Playback, around 2014 (the article is undated online)

When Deluxe closed its west-end Toronto lab, it left a lab business it had been in locally since the 1960s: "The economics and the demand no longer made sense," said Deluxe Toronto's Dan McLellan. Technicolor and Deluxe had both shut their Canadian labs, leaving Vision Globale in Montréal. The article says widespread digital projection is what ultimately hurt film shooting. Digital jobs grew: Deluxe Toronto had a data input and output department of six staff and 15 people encoding content for platforms. A Technicolor Canada executive said the importance of skilled colourists had not changed.

💾 The digital footprint: what we know and what we don't

Film is gone, but post still burns energy: storage, rendering, cloud review and streaming. The honest summary from the sources below is that the viewing end is small per hour, Canadian data shows post as a minor slice, and nobody has yet measured cloud storage and rendering for screen production properly. When a Canadian study does, it goes here.

Carbon impact of video streaming

The Carbon Trust white paper, with DIMPACT (funded by Netflix)

Puts one hour of video-on-demand streaming at about 55 g of CO2e in Europe, which it calls very small next to other everyday activities. The viewing device is typically the biggest part of the footprint, and moving from high to standard definition made only a very small difference. Note the funder: Netflix paid for the work.

Telefilm spotlights Canadian film and TV's carbon footprint

Playback, February 22, 2024

Telefilm's study of 22 Canadian productions, covering 144 hours of content, found TV series averaged 34 tonnes of CO2 per hour and features 25. Accommodation, Post-Production and disposal together made up about 10% of average emissions. Features under $1 million averaged 12 tonnes per hour against 59 for those over $5 million. In Canadian data, post is a small slice. The slice may be growing as work moves to the cloud, and the study did not isolate it.

albert: year one report, carbon footprinting the TV industry

BAFTA albert, UK

In the UK's first-year data, an hour of TV production averaged 5.8 tonnes of CO2e. Travel and the production office made up 68% of that, and accommodation another tonne. Location work and Post-Production make up the last part. It is UK television, not Canadian, and from the early years of the tool.

Virtual production's role: a carbon study

Studio Ulster at Ulster University

A report on virtual production says the carbon footprint of extensive cloud computing, rendering and data storage is not well understood in the creative industries. It argues the movement of data could be a very large source of emissions but that nobody yet knows the scale, and calls for a carbon calculator built for the job. It also found that virtual production cut emissions in its tests.

Honest scorecard

Five provinces have their own green-production programs and the DGC runs one of the most detailed guild hubs, but the UK's albert calculator is still the benchmark that Canadian programs build on. Canada has real momentum here. Calling it the world leader would be an overstatement, and the one gap everyone admits is data on Post-Production and cloud rendering.

Where post fits

Start with the Editors Top Tips on DGC Green. Beyond that, the biggest levers in post are compute and storage: where your cloud or render farm gets its power, how long machines run idle, and how much media you keep and move. Ask your facility for its numbers.
