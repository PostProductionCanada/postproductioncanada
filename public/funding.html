💰 Funding and Tax Credits

Where Canadian screen money comes from and where it lands. Researched from public sources, listed at the bottom. This is information, not legal or tax advice.

Funding and tax credit changes that matter to post, Visual Effects and animation, newest first. Each line links to the page we read.

The $600 million: who gets what

On November 8, 2026, after Budget 2026, Culture Minister Marc Miller announced how the $600 million for Canada's audio and audiovisual sectors will be handed out. The government says the money is meant to provide stability while longer-term policy, regulatory and market conditions evolve. The list below is Canadian Heritage's own, in the order of the size of each share.

What is not settled

  • How long it lasts. In June the government said it would provide $600 million in annual funding to replace payments from big streamers that the CRTC ordered under the Online Streaming Act. The Canadian Press reports the federal budget included only one year of the enhanced funding, and a government spokesperson said future amounts could vary once the new CRTC rules are final. (Canadian Press, via Yahoo)
  • What an analyst says. Policy Options (Nov 24) reports the minister's position that, "once the new CRTC rules are finalized, the level of government investment will be adjusted as appropriate." Its author writes that the whole $600 million will not last, because it could be reduced by the amount streamers are required to reinvest in Canadian programming. (Policy Options)
  • What it means for post. The backgrounder does not name Post-Production. Most of the money goes to funds that pay for production and promotion, so the effect on post work will depend on how each fund writes its new programs. We have not seen those details yet.
  • Telefilm. Telefilm said on Nov 12 that its teams are mobilized to put the additional $68.5 million to work. (Telefilm Canada on Facebook) We will add details here once Telefilm publishes them.

Funding agencies for filmmakers and game makers

Every screen funder we could find in Canada, federal, provincial and territorial, with a link to the agency itself. Search by name or place, or filter by what you make. We describe each one as its own pages do. We leave out deadlines and amounts that change with each intake, so open the link for the current call.

Telefilm Canada

Federal

Federal funder of Canadian feature films. Its Production Program finances features at the production or Post-Production stage, and it runs separate Indigenous development and production streams.

Production ProgramIndigenous funding

Canada Media Fund (CMF)

Federal

Funds Canadian television and digital content, and runs the main federal programs for interactive projects. Its program list covers linear production, plus interactive digital media programs.

All CMF programsProgram deadlines

CMF, game and interactive programs

Federal

The CMF's interactive digital media streams: Conceptualization, Prototyping, Innovation & Experimentation (software applications that are innovative and leading-edge) and Commercial Projects (projects with a greater probability of commercial success), plus the CMF and Creative BC Video Games Business Development Program for B.C.-owned and controlled game companies.

PrototypingInnovation & ExperimentationCommercial ProjectsVideo Games Business Development

National Film Board: Filmmaker Assistance Program

Federal

Reimburses selected independent filmmakers for Post-Production costs. The page sets the cap at $7,500 per filmmaker for a short film and $15,000 for a feature. Filmmakers in Montreal or within 150 km of it can instead get Post-Production services in NFB studios, paying 10% of the cost.

FAP program page

Canada Council for the Arts: Explore and Create

Federal

Grants for research, development, creation, production and sharing of work, including media and digital arts, plus professional development. The program page shows film and documentary projects among its funded recipients.

Explore and Create

Indigenous Screen Office (ISO)

National

Funds Indigenous screen creators and Indigenous-owned production companies, with development and production programs. Its page also points to outside funders.

ISO funding opportunities

Black Screen Office (BSO)

National

Non-profit that says it exists to champion Black creators, grow Black-led businesses and make sure Black stories are made and seen. Check its site for current programs.

Black Screen Office

CAVCO tax credits (Canadian Heritage)

Federal

Federal tax credits: the Canadian Film or Video Production Tax Credit and the Film or Video Production Services Tax Credit. These are credits against tax, not grants, and are claimed by production companies.

Canadian Film or Video Production Tax Credit

Bell Fund

National

Supports Canadian independent producers. Its funding programs include a Production Program for Canadian series and a Slate Development program for original IP.

Funding programs

B.C. and Alberta

Funding, training and distribution for new and emerging creators in B.C. and Alberta, including short films and non-fiction series. Its homepage lists each current edition and what it pays.

STORYHIVE

Canadian Independent Screen Fund

National

A funding database aimed at independent filmmakers, listing programs by agency and showing the eligibility rules for each.

Canadian Independent Screen Fund

Ontario Creates

Ontario

The province's agency for film and television, interactive digital media, book, magazine and music. It runs funding programs and administers Ontario's film and interactive tax credits. Applications go through its Online Application Portal.

Ontario CreatesFilm and TV tax credit (OFTTC)

Ontario Creates: IP Fund, Interactive Stream

Ontario

Ontario Creates funds video games and XR experiences through its IP Fund, with extra support from Global Market Development, Futures Forward and Industry Development programs. The 2026-27 guidelines list two intake deadlines a year.

Interactive sector pageInteractive digital media page

Creative BC

British Columbia

B.C.'s agency for motion picture, interactive digital media, music, book and magazine. It runs grants for creators and companies, the film commission and the provincial motion picture tax credits.

Funding programsCreative BC

Creative BC: games and interactive

British Columbia

The Interactive Fund (with the BC Arts Council) is for interactive digital media projects; its FAQ says conventional, primarily commercial games are not eligible this year because games have their own funds. Those are the Video Games Business Development and Video Games Early Stage Growth programs. When we read the pages, the Interactive Fund and Early Stage Growth pilot (grants up to $50,000) showed as closed.

Interactive FundEarly Stage Growth

Alberta Media Fund

Alberta

Grants for screen-based production, including the Alberta Made Production Grant for smaller-budget productions and a Post-Production Grant. Alberta's funding page says the Post-Production Grant covers up to 18% of post cost, including visual effects and digital animation, to a maximum of $200,000 a year. Its Project Script Development Grant lists console and online video games as ineligible.

Alberta Media FundFunding and incentives for filmAlberta Made Production Grant

Edmonton Screen Industries Office

Alberta (Edmonton)

Funding and support for television and interactive digital media productions in the Metro Edmonton area, through the Edmonton Screen Media Fund.

Edmonton Screen Industries

Creative Saskatchewan

Saskatchewan

Provincial agency that invests in the creative industries. Its film and TV support covers feature and television production, and its Digital Game Development Grant has a pre-production and a production stream, with grants of up to $50,000 or 50% of the cash budget a fiscal year, whichever is less (51% Saskatchewan ownership is required).

Film and TV productionDigital Game Development

Manitoba Film & Music

Manitoba

Manitoba's film commission, administrator of the Manitoba Film and Video Production Tax Credit, and a funder of film and music projects. It has also run a Manitoba Indigenous Short Film Fund with the Indigenous Screen Office.

Manitoba Film & Music

SODEC

Québec

Québec's agency for cultural enterprises. It supports script development, production and marketing of films, and administers Québec's film and television tax credits. Its site is mostly in French.

SODEC

PictureNL

Newfoundland and Labrador

The province's lead screen agency. Its Equity Investment Program invests in Newfoundland and Labrador productions; the investment is recoupable and can be combined with the provincial film tax credit.

Equity Investment Program

Nova Scotia

The province's incentive fund for film and television production. The industry association Screen Nova Scotia runs a Content Creator Fund as a top-up for qualifying Nova Scotia productions.

Incentive FundScreen Nova Scotia

Interactive digital media tax credits

By province

Provincial tax credits for game and interactive studios. Ontario's OIDMTC is a refundable credit of up to 40% of eligible Ontario spending. Québec's multimedia credit is up to 37.5% of eligible labour, with Investissement Québec issuing the eligibility certificate. B.C., Manitoba, Newfoundland and Labrador and Nova Scotia each publish their own credit.

OntarioQuébecB.C.ManitobaNewfoundland and LabradorNova Scotia

Read on each agency's own pages in November 2026. Programs, rules and open dates change often, and some programs showed as closed or between intakes when we looked. If we missed a funder, tell us through the contact page. This is information, not advice.

Production tax credits, federal and by province

These credits go to production companies, not to individual Editors. They matter because they decide where work lands, and some target post directly: BC's DAVE credit covers Post-Production and VFX labour, and Saskatchewan adds a bonus for post done in-province.

Credits for VFX, animation and post

Three places run credits aimed squarely at visual effects, animation and Post-Production work: British Columbia, Ontario and Québec. These go to production and post companies, not individual artists, but they decide where VFX and post work gets booked.

WhereWhat it addsOfficial sources
FederalFilm or Video Production Services Tax Credit (PSTC): 16% of qualified Canadian labour. No VFX-specific bonus.PSTC (CAVCO)
British ColumbiaDAVE: 16% of eligible BC labour directly attributable to digital animation, visual effects and Post-Production. It is a bonus on top of the basic PSTC (36%) or Film Incentive BC (40%), and you must claim one of those first. Entertainment Partners notes that PSTC plus DAVE is available to productions with only VFX and post work done in BC, with no principal photography requirement.DAVE bulletin (Creative BC) · PSTC rates (Creative BC)
OntarioOCASE: 18% of eligible Ontario labour on computer animation and special effects, no cap, claimed on top of the OFTTC (35%) or OPSTC (21.5%). Animation studios, VFX houses, post houses and 3D service providers can qualify. Ontario labour must exceed $25,000. Eligibility depends on several tests, so it is not a simple parallel to BC.OCASE (Ontario Creates)
QuébecOn top of the Production Services Tax Credit (25% of qualified spend), an extra 16% on qualified labour for computer-aided special effects and animation, including chroma-key shooting. For service contracts involving computer-aided special effects and animation, SODEC's fact sheet gives an effective rate of 26.65%, worked out as (25% + 16%) × 65%. The domestic credit has a smaller bonus, reported at 10% with a cap.Fact sheet (SODEC, March 2026) · Overview (Entertainment Partners) · Animation and effects credits (Time Consulting)
Other provincesWe found no VFX-specific credit in Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, the Atlantic provinces or the territories. Their general credits may still cover post spend, so check each program.CMPA overview

Rates checked against Creative BC and Ontario Creates on Nov 18, 2026. Budgets change these, so confirm with the agency before you plan around a figure.
