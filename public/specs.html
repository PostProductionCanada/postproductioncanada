📐 Delivery Specs and Tech

What buyers actually ask for, the loudness targets, and how much drive you need.

A delivery spec is the buyer's rulebook for what you hand over: the file format, resolution, frame rate, audio layout, colour and paperwork. Every network and streamer has its own, they change, and the one that counts is always the version your buyer sends you. This page points to where the public ones live, then gives you two reference charts you can use at the start of a job: which codec is which, and how much footage a drive holds.

Facts read on November 20, 2026. Delivery specs change, so always confirm with the buyer.

Where the delivery specs live

CBC/Radio-Canada: technical specifications for program delivery

CBC/Radio-Canada

The public program delivery document. The English version we found is Version 5.1 from September 2016. A French version, 6.0 from December 2020, was prepared by CBC/Radio-Canada's UHD/HD/SD working group, so the English file may be out of date. Ask CBC for the version in force on your show.

CBC/Radio-Canada: normes techniques de livraison des programmes, version 6.0

CBC/Radio-Canada, December 2, 2020 (French)

The newer program delivery standards, covering UHD, HD and SD.

Bell Media video specifications

Bell Media (file dated January 2026 in its web address)

Bell Media's published video specifications for delivery, in English. Confirm with your Bell Media contact which edition applies.

Netflix Backlot help center

Netflix

Netflix publishes its specs here, including the IMF specification (Version 5.1) and its servicing and turnover pages. Netflix describes IMF as the final delivery format for originals, made by a Netflix-approved vendor, and its Content Hub pages list ProRes 422 HQ for servicing turnovers. Netflix notes some specs may not apply to every production, so confirm with your Netflix post team.

Prime Video: video requirements

Amazon Prime Video

Amazon's video delivery requirements for its Prime Video catalogue.

What is normal in Canadian delivery

Read straight from the buyers' own documents. The common thread: a broadcast-quality 4:2:2 file, ProRes HQ or XDCAM HD at 50 Mbps, at 1080, in the frame rate the show was shot.

🔊 Audio specs

The same pattern for sound: 48 kHz and 24-bit everywhere, a peak limit of -2, and a loudness target that differs by buyer. Canadian broadcasters ask for -24 LKFS; Netflix measures dialogue only and asks for -27. Mixing to the wrong one is the classic delivery rejection.

BuyerSample rate and bit depthLoudnessPeakTracks
CBC/Radio-Canada (v5.1, 2016)PCM, 24-bit, 48 kHz-24 LKFS +/- 2 LU, integrated and dialogue-2 dBTP maximum2 tracks (stereo) up to 6 (5.1), plus described video. CBC layout: 8 channels (L, R, C, LFE, Ls, Rs, DV-L, DV-R)
Bell Media (revision November 2025)48 kHz, 24-bit-24 LKFS +/- 2 LU (ITU-R BS.1770-4)-2 dBFS maximumSurround and stereo track layouts are specified in its document
Netflix (sound mix specs v1.5)48 kHz, 24-bit, discrete LPCM .wav or .bwav; no lossy audio-27 LKFS +/- 2 LU, dialogue-gated (ITU-R BS.1770-1)-2 dB true peak maximum5.1 or 2.0 near-field mixes, with stems that add up to the mix

LKFS is the loudness unit; LU is the tolerance around the target. Check the spec for how each buyer measures it. These tables come from the documents linked above, as read on November 20, 2026. A newer version may exist, so ask your buyer.

Further reading: Mixer Talks Tools for TV Docs (Mix, June 4, 2026). A UK re-recording mixer on keeping documentary mixes inside loudness specs and upmixing library music into surround. It is a profile of Nugen Audio's plug-ins, so read it as a vendor-friendly view, and it is not Canadian.

🎞️ Codec chart

A codec is how the picture is squeezed into a file. These are the two main editing and finishing families. Higher data rate means bigger files and more headroom for grading and VFX.

XDCAM and Apple ProRes, at 1920 x 1080 and 29.97 fps (⭐ = named in the delivery specs we read)

CodecWhat it is forData ratePer hour
XDCAM HD422 (50 Mbps) ⭐CBC's preferred delivery format and Bell Media's broadcast-ready format (MXF)50 Mbps22 GB
ProRes 422 ProxyOffline editing at full resolution45 Mbps20 GB
ProRes 422 LTSmaller files for editing and some delivery102 Mbps46 GB
ProRes 422Everyday editing and finishing147 Mbps66 GB
ProRes 422 HQ ⭐High-quality 4:2:2 finishing and mastering; Bell Media's first choice for HD and UHD delivery220 Mbps99 GB
ProRes 44444:4:4 sources, graphics and alpha channels, nearly lossless330 Mbps148 GB
ProRes 4444 XQThe highest-quality 4:4:4 for demanding colour and VFX work500 Mbps225 GB

Data rates are Apple's target rates from its ProRes documentation, and the 50 Mbps for XDCAM HD422 comes from CBC's spec. "Per hour" is our arithmetic from that rate (Mbps x 3,600 seconds, divided by 8, in decimal gigabytes). Real files vary a little, and UHD files are much bigger: UHD has four times the pixels of 1080p.

What is not here yet

Delivery codecs such as H.264 and H.265, and camera RAW formats, vary too much by camera and settings for a single chart, and we have not found sources we trust for them yet. Tell us which you want next, and send any delivery spec you would like listed, at hello@postproductioncanada.ca.
