📊 By the Numbers

Canada's post industry in charts, from Statistics Canada and Telefilm Canada.

$2.4B💵 Post-Production revenue, 2025
−14.7%📉 vs the 2023 record
+18.7%📈 vs 2019, before the pandemic
6.7%🧮 operating profit margin (6.0% in 2023)

Canada's Post-Production industry earned $2.4 billion in 2025. That is down from a record 2023 but still well above 2019. The change was uneven: British Columbia gained while Quebec fell. Every chart below comes from Statistics Canada or Telefilm Canada's own reporting.

🎬 The production side, which feeds post

Post work follows shooting by months, so production figures show what may reach post rooms next. StatCan makes the same point: its production survey for 2025 is due in early 2027. These numbers are from Telefilm Canada's 2025–26 annual report, as reported by Broadcast Dialogue. They are about production, not post.

🎮 Gaming: big money next door to post

Gaming uses the same skills as screen work: cinematics, VFX, animation, sound and Editors. These figures are from the gaming industry's own association, so read them as the industry's numbers.

$5.1B🏦 contribution to Canada's GDP, 2024
34,010👷 full-time-equivalent jobs
821🕹️ active video game studios
$102,000💼 average salary

Source: ESAC economic impact study, released Jan. 28, 2025 (CNW) · Video Games Canada (ESAC)

🎮 Rockstar says: GTA VI sold $3.79 billion on day one

Rockstar Games reported $3.79 billion in "day one retail sales" for Grand Theft Auto VI through Nov. 19, 2026, and 32.5 million units sold before release. It calls this the biggest entertainment launch in history. This is Rockstar's own internal estimate, it includes pre-sales, and it is one game's retail sales, so it is not comparable to the GDP figure above. We have not confirmed where any of the work was done.

Source: The Hollywood Reporter, on Rockstar's announcement

✅ How we check these

We publish a figure only if we read it on the publisher's own page, and the page is linked beside it. We do not estimate, project or round. When we work something out ourselves, such as a remainder, the chart says "our arithmetic". When a page leaves something out, we leave it out too. Figures were read on November 21, 2026.

The Post-Production numbers are Statistics Canada's biennial survey, with 2021 and 2023 data revised. The next post release is scheduled for fall 2028. Spot an error? Email hello@postproductioncanada.ca with the page that shows it.
