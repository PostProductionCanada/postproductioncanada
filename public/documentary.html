🎥 Documentary in Canada

Canadians have been telling their own stories on film for more than a century. Here is how it happened.

More than a century, in ten steps

Every step links to its source. This is a short list, not a full history.

  1. 1897 📍 Manitoba

    James Freer, an English-born settler in Manitoba, gets equipment and films the Canadian West. His Ten Years in Manitoba is the first known film by a Canadian, made partly to attract British settlers.

    Source
  2. 1918 📍 Canada

    The federal government creates the Exhibits and Publicity Bureau, renamed the Canadian Government Motion Picture Bureau in 1923. By 1920 it ran the largest studio and Post-Production facility in Canada and made the Seeing Canada travelogue series.

    Source
  3. 1920s–30s 📍 Québec

    Associated Screen News of Montréal, backed by the CPR, makes newsreels and shorts, including the Canadian Cameo series (1932–53). In Québec, priests Albert Tessier and Maurice Proulx film rural life, work considered early documentary.

    Source
  4. 1939 📍 Canada

    The National Film Act is given royal assent on May 2 and creates the National Film Board. John Grierson, who coined the word “documentary”, becomes its first commissioner.

    Source
  5. 1940s 📍 Canada

    Wartime series such as Canada Carries On and The World in Action reach audiences of millions.

    Source
  6. 1950s 📍 Canada

    Young NFB filmmakers in Unit B make Corral (1954), City of Gold (1957) and Lonely Boy (1961), and the Candid Eye series for TV. Lightweight 16mm cameras and portable sound change how documentaries are shot.

    Source
  7. 1956–63 📍 Québec

    The NFB moves to Montréal. Direct cinema flowers in Québec with Les Raquetteurs (1958), La Lutte (1961) and Pour la suite du monde (1963) by Pierre Perrault IMDb and Michel Brault IMDb.

    Source
  8. 1967–80 📍 Newfoundland and Labrador📍 Canada

    Challenge for Change puts film and video in the hands of communities. Over 200 films come out of it, including 27 about Fogo Island, Newfoundland. The Indian Film Crew is Canada’s first all-Indigenous production unit.

    Source
  9. 1993 📍 Ontario

    Hot Docs is founded by the Documentary Organization of Canada. It is now the largest documentary festival in North America.

    Source
  10. Today 📍 Canada

    A distinct Indigenous cinematic voice has emerged, and Canadian documentary is made on video by independents as much as at the NFB. Editors are honoured with their own documentary categories at the CCE, Canadian Screen and DGC Awards.

    Source
Why the Editor matters most here.

In documentary there is no script to follow. The story is found in the cutting room, and the Editor shapes it from hours of real footage. A longer original article on this history is in the members section.

Legends who still shape Canadian film and TV

Each name links to Wikipedia or IMDb. Know a name that belongs here? Send it with a source.

  • Norman McLaren
    NFB animator. His film Neighbours won the Academy Award for Best Documentary Short.
  • Colin Low
    NFB filmmaker behind Corral (1954) and, with Wolf Koenig, City of Gold (1957).
  • Roman Kroitor
    Co-directed Lonely Boy (1961) and later co-founded IMAX.
  • Michel Brault
    Cinematographer and director, and Pierre Perrault's partner on Pour la suite du monde (1963).
  • Pierre Perrault
    Québec poet and filmmaker, a founder of direct cinema in Canada.
  • Allan King
    Documentary director of Warrendale (1967).
  • Evelyn Lambart
    NFB animator who worked alongside Norman McLaren IMDb.
  • Nell Shipman
    Silent-era actor, writer and director, and an early Canadian woman filmmaker.
  • David Cronenberg
    Toronto director whose films reshaped body horror and Canadian cinema.
  • Ronald Sanders
    Editor of David Cronenberg IMDb's films.
  • Denis Villeneuve
    Québec director of Incendies, Arrival and Dune.
  • Jean-Marc Vallée
    Québec director and Editor of Dallas Buyers Club and Big Little Lies, who died in 2021.

Where to watch and learn

National Film Board of Canada

Streams a large catalogue of Canadian documentary for free.

Hot Docs

The Toronto festival, with the Hot Docs Ted Rogers Cinema. The 2027 festival runs April 23 to May 2.

Documentary Organization of Canada

The national association of independent documentary makers. It also lists mental health resources for filmmakers.

Awards for documentary Editors

CCE, Canadian Screen and DGC documentary editing winners.
